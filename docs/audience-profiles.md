# Audience profiles (ICPs) for the guides

Who actually benefits from each guide, the moment they need it, and where to
reach them. This is a distribution and prioritization tool — the site is a free
public good, so "customer" here means the ideal *reader to reach*, not a buyer.

Last updated: 2026-06. Owner: editorial. Treat the channel/association lists as
a starting map, not verified market data; the sizing and channel-performance
numbers are the obvious next research step.

---

## The reach model (two audience types, two strategies)

Everything on the site serves one of two kinds of reader, and they are reached
very differently:

1. **People in the moment of need** — parents, survivors, people worried about
   their own behavior. They arrive via **search**, at a crisis or decision
   point, often at 1 a.m., often ashamed. Strategy: own the search queries, and
   be linked from the crisis channels they already trust (NCMEC, RAINN, 988,
   Take It Down, pediatricians).

2. **Professional multipliers** — therapists, attorneys, educators, PTA leaders,
   tech leaders, journalists. Each one reaches *many* at-risk people. Strategy:
   reach them through their **professional associations, referral networks, and
   conferences**. One therapist or one journalist is worth thousands of
   search hits in downstream impact.

Prioritize multipliers for outreach effort; prioritize in-the-moment audiences
for SEO and crisis-partner linking. Most guides are one type cleanly; a few
(reporting) are cross-cutting.

## Per-guide profiles

Format: **Who** · **Trigger** (the moment) · **Core question** · **What the
guide gives** · **Where to reach them** · **Leverage**.

### For Parents — *in-the-moment*
- **Who:** A parent/caregiver of a child roughly 6–17.
- **Trigger:** "I just found porn / a sextortion DM / a deepfake on my kid's phone," or a school just sent a scary email.
- **Core question:** "What do I do right now, and how do I talk to my kid without making it worse?"
- **Gives:** Age-by-age scripts, sextortion warning signs, device setup, a calm what-to-do path.
- **Reach:** Search ("my kid saw porn," "sextortion help teen"); pediatrician and school-counselor handouts; Common Sense Media-adjacent communities; parenting subreddits/Facebook groups.
- **Leverage:** Low per-contact (one family) but the largest total audience; the SEO anchor of the site.

### For PTA Groups — *multiplier*
- **Who:** PTA/PTO presidents and boards; room parents; parent-group organizers.
- **Trigger:** Scared parents keep cornering them for answers; they're asked to "do something" / run an AI-safety night.
- **Core question:** "How do I help my *whole* parent community without being an expert?"
- **Gives:** A ready-to-run parent-night agenda, the questions parents ask with short answers, a one-page resource set.
- **Reach:** National PTA + state/local PTA chapters; school-admin and principal newsletters; the parent-education / family-engagement conference circuit.
- **Leverage:** High — one PTA president reaches a whole school's families.

### For Survivors — *in-the-moment*
- **Who:** People (any age) who experienced sexual abuse, image-based abuse, sextortion, or grooming — recent or historical.
- **Trigger:** An image is circulating; a memory surfaces; they're deciding whether to report or get help.
- **Core question:** "Can this be removed, and where do I find help that gets it?"
- **Gives:** Take It Down / StopNCII, honest removal expectations, trauma-informed care directories.
- **Reach:** NCMEC/Take It Down, RAINN, Cybertip referrals; therapist referrals; search. Linking from the clearinghouses is the highest-value placement.
- **Leverage:** Low per-contact, high stakes; reputationally central — this guide must be impeccable.

### For Educators — *multiplier*
- **Who:** Teachers, school counselors, administrators, school IT.
- **Trigger:** An incident lands on their desk (a deepfake ring, a disclosure, porn on a school device) and they must act today.
- **Core question:** "What are my first steps and mandatory-reporting obligations — without making it worse or investigating illegally?"
- **Gives:** Six scenario playbooks, sample language, the believe-the-student / don't-investigate-alone rules.
- **Reach:** NEA/AFT, ASCA (school counselors), district professional-development days, edtech-safety and CoSN/ISTE channels, state education agencies.
- **Leverage:** High — one counselor handles many students/year.

### For People Seeking Help — *in-the-moment (hardest to reach)*
- **Who:** People worried about their own sexual thoughts or use, struggling with CSAM use, or pre-charge.
- **Trigger:** Shame spike; a near-miss; fear of getting caught.
- **Core question:** "Can I get help without destroying my life / triggering a report?"
- **Gives:** Confidentiality realities, anonymous prevention services (Troubled Desire, Stop It Now!), evidence-based treatment, attorney-first guidance.
- **Reach:** Anonymous search; partnerships with prevention helplines (Stop It Now!, Lucy Faithfull, Troubled Desire); discreet placement — this audience won't engage via "social" channels.
- **Leverage:** Disproportionate *prevention* value per reader; politically sensitive but mission-critical.

### For Therapists — *multiplier*
- **Who:** Psychologists, LMFTs, LCSWs, psychiatrists, counselors.
- **Trigger:** A patient discloses PPU, a minor-attraction concern, or active legal exposure and they feel under-equipped.
- **Core question:** "How do I screen, handle confidentiality/mandatory reporting, and treat this with the evidence base?"
- **Gives:** Intake screening, the session-one confidentiality script, CBT/ACT effect sizes, the therapeutic-vs-forensic line.
- **Reach:** AASECT, ATSA (offense-specific), APA divisions, Psychology Today / clinician directories, CE/CEU providers (a CE module is a strong wedge).
- **Leverage:** Very high — each clinician treats many patients; also lends the site clinical credibility.

### For Attorneys — *multiplier*
- **Who:** Criminal defense counsel and prosecutors in CSAM/online-exploitation cases.
- **Trigger:** They pick up a case and need defensible, current evidence on recidivism, risk tools, treatment, and AI-CSAM law.
- **Core question:** "What does the evidence actually say, with the caveats that survive cross-examination?"
- **Gives:** Recidivism data with the self-report gap, Static-99R/Stable vs AASI controversies, treatment-for-mitigation, the ENFORCE/TAKE IT DOWN landscape.
- **Reach:** NACDL, state criminal-defense bars, public-defender offices, CLE providers, sentencing-mitigation networks.
- **Leverage:** High per case; an authority/credibility halo (lawyers cite carefully).

### For Tech CEOs — *multiplier*
- **Who:** Founders, CTOs, heads of trust & safety at platforms hosting user content or building generative AI.
- **Trigger:** Launch, an incident, an investor/acquirer diligence request, or a new law.
- **Core question:** "What does the legal floor actually require, in what order, and where am I personally exposed?"
- **Gives:** The § 2258A floor, the minimum viable stack, where-accountability-sits, personal-liability.
- **Reach:** Tech Coalition, the Trust & Safety Professional Association (TSPA), accelerators (YC/a16z safety programming), founder communities.
- **Leverage:** Very high — one decision protects an entire platform's users.

### For Developers — *multiplier*
- **Who:** Engineers implementing detection/safety at those platforms.
- **Trigger:** They're told to "add CSAM detection" and don't know how, safely.
- **Core question:** "How do I wire this up — and not mishandle what I find?"
- **Gives:** The pipeline, hashing/matching, the no-copy handling rule, testing without real material, the OSS toolkit.
- **Reach:** TSPA, GitHub (the fight-csam repo is the funnel), Hacker News / dev communities, the tools page.
- **Leverage:** High; also the OSS-adoption growth loop.

### For Compliance Teams — *multiplier*
- **Who:** Compliance officers, T&S ops, legal, internal audit.
- **Trigger:** An audit, a regulator (DSA/OSA/eSafety), or enterprise-customer diligence.
- **Core question:** "What do I document, and what will an assessor ask to see?"
- **Gives:** Multi-jurisdiction map, the audit checklist, metrics that hold up, governance/accountability, retention.
- **Reach:** TSPA, IAPP (privacy/compliance overlap), DSA/OSA compliance forums, RegTech circles.
- **Leverage:** High; pairs with Tech CEOs and Developers as the platform trio.

### For Reporting Content — *cross-cutting task guide*
- **Who:** Anyone who has found CSAM/abuse and needs to report it (overlaps every audience above).
- **Trigger:** "I just saw this — where do I report it?"
- **Core question:** "Where do I report on *this* platform, and what's safe to do?"
- **Gives:** 114-platform directory + clearinghouses + the don't-download safety rules.
- **Reach:** Surfaced from Get Help, search ("how to report X"), and linked from every other guide.
- **Leverage:** Utility infrastructure; not an outreach target on its own.

## Prioritization (leverage × reachability)

- **Tier 1 — pursue actively (high leverage, reachable via associations):**
  For Therapists, For Educators, For PTA Groups, For Tech CEOs. Each multiplies,
  and each has a clear association/conference door to knock on.
- **Tier 2 — own the search + crisis-partner links:** For Parents, For Survivors,
  For Reporting. Highest total reach; win by SEO and clearinghouse linking, not
  outreach.
- **Tier 3 — specialist, partner-gated:** For Attorneys, For Compliance Teams,
  For Developers, For People Seeking Help. High value but reached through narrow,
  often gated channels (bars, TSPA, prevention helplines).

## Gap analysis — candidate new audiences

### For the Press / Journalists — **recommend: BUILD (not a distraction)**
The highest-leverage gap. Reporters covering school-deepfake incidents, new
laws, platform scandals, and NCMEC data shape public understanding *and* policy,
usually on deadline, and this beat is notoriously mis-covered: wrong terminology
("child porn"), misused statistics, and republished or over-described material.
The site is uniquely positioned to fix that — it already has the terminology
discipline (`/editorial-standards`) and the single most-misreported caveat (the
Stanford CIS finding that most "generative-AI" CyberTipline reports are *not*
AI-generated CSAM).

- **Who:** Reporters, editors, producers, fact-checkers on the child-safety / AI / tech-policy / education / crime beats; plus freelancers parachuting in on a breaking story.
- **Trigger:** A story breaks; they need accurate framing in two hours.
- **Core question:** "How do I cover this accurately and responsibly without causing harm or getting the terms and numbers wrong?"
- **Should give:** Correct terminology and why it matters; the statistics *with* their caveats; victim-centered language; a clear "do not publish / do not describe" list (methods, app names, identifying details, images); where to verify (NCMEC, IWF, the citations page); and named expert-source pathways. Anchor it to established frameworks — the **Dart Center for Journalism & Trauma**, **RAINN's media guidelines**, and **IWF / Lucy Faithfull** media guidance.
- **Reach:** Dart Center, SPJ, science/tech-journalism networks; a linkable "for press" / media-kit page; proactive outreach to beat reporters when relevant news breaks.
- **Why it's worth it:** One article reaches 10k–1M people; good coverage is high-leverage *prevention* (less copycat behavior, less moral panic, better-informed policy), and journalists cite sources — earning authority and inbound links. Framing matters: build it as **responsible-reporting guidance, not a PR page**.

### Other candidates (quick verdicts)
- **Pediatricians / family physicians — build later (fold into a clinical push).** They are a trusted, high-frequency channel to parents (well-child visits) and could hand out For Parents. Reach via AAP. A light "for clinicians who see families" angle, or just a printable parent handout, may be enough before a full guide.
- **School counselors — already covered.** Keep inside For Educators; don't split.
- **Faith & youth-org leaders (coaches, scout/club leaders, camp staff) — maybe.** Large trusted-adult surface for grooming prevention, but fragmented and hard to reach at scale. Watch; not a priority guide yet.
- **Policymakers / legislative staff — partially covered by `/laws`.** A dedicated guide risks looking advocacy-driven; better served by keeping the laws tracker and citations impeccable than by a "For Policymakers" guide.
- **Librarians — skip for now.** Real trusted-adult role, but small marginal reach over Parents/Educators.

**Net recommendation:** build **For the Press** next (Tier-1 leverage, low build cost, strong fit). Hold the rest; revisit pediatricians as part of a clinical-channel push alongside For Therapists.
